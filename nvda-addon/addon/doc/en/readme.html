AudioNet for NVDA

AudioNet moves live audio between your computers, phones and browsers. With this add-on, NVDA listens to your AudioNet devices on this computer, and sends this computer's microphone or sound to them.

NVDA is a visitor, not a device

NVDA joins your accounts the way the AudioNet web client does. It is not added to your account as a device, so it does not appear in your device list, and your other devices cannot listen to it or send to it. It can listen to your devices and send to them. To make this computer a device of your account instead, install the AudioNet app for Windows.

Adding an account

Open NVDA's settings (NVDA+N, Preferences, Settings) and choose the AudioNet category, then Add account. Enter your server address, your account name and your password. Your password is used once to sign in and is never kept; NVDA keeps only its sign-in, encrypted for your Windows user. You can add several accounts. Removing an account signs NVDA out of it; your devices and the account are not affected.

You need an AudioNet account first: create one in your server's web client. Forgot your password? The Forgot password button opens your server's password reset page.

The AudioNet window

Open it from NVDA's Tools menu (AudioNet...), or press NVDA+Alt+A and then W. It has:

  • Account: which account's devices to show, with its connection state.
  • Devices: a tree. Each device says whether it is online and sharing. Expand it for Sounds to listen to (a device shares its sounds only when sharing is on) and Outputs to send to. Press Enter on a sound to listen to it, or on an output to send to it.
  • Play on this computer: where what you listen to plays.
  • Send from this computer: which microphone or sound you send.
  • Listen and Send: the same as Enter in the tree.
  • Streams: the streams you started, with their state. Select one to Stop it, or change its Volume and Mute (on this computer: what it plays here, or what it sends from here).
  • Status log: what happened, and each stream's measurements in words.
  • Accounts: opens the AudioNet settings.

Closing the window keeps your streams running.

Keyboard commands

Press NVDA+Alt+A to turn on AudioNet commands, then any of these keys. The commands stay on, so you can press several (the volume a few steps, then a report), until you press Escape, press NVDA+Alt+A again, or open the AudioNet window with W or O.

  • W or O: open the AudioNet window.
  • L: listen again to the sound you last listened to.
  • S: stop all streams.
  • R: report the streams and their state.
  • M: mute or unmute all streams.
  • Up arrow and down arrow: all streams' volume, 10 percent at a time.
  • D: report which devices are online.
  • H: list these keys.
  • Escape: turn AudioNet commands off.

Each of these commands can also have its own key: open Input Gestures (NVDA+N, Preferences, Input gestures) and look under AudioNet. NVDA+Alt+A can be changed there too.

Announcements

NVDA announces when a stream connects and when it ends for a reason other than your stopping it. In the AudioNet settings you can turn that off, or turn on announcements when devices go online or offline.

Privacy and security

Audio is always encrypted, and goes straight between this computer and your device (through your server's relay only when a direct path is impossible, and still encrypted). The AudioNet engine runs as a separate program started by NVDA (audionet-visitor.exe), so streaming never slows NVDA down; it stops when NVDA exits. The add-on does nothing on secure screens.
