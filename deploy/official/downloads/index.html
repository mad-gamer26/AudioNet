Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit)

AudioNet 0.8.1 for Windows, zip file, about 9 megabytes

SHA-256 checksum: 2e15a509108487afb55637f9104c70589400deb163dffbd1c965bd7994bef860

Install

  1. Download the zip file and extract it to a folder, for example your Documents folder.
  2. Run audionet-desktop.exe. The programs are not yet code-signed, so Windows SmartScreen may ask for confirmation.
  3. In the app, check the server address, enter your AudioNet account name and password, press Sign in. The computer is then online in your account; press Start sharing to let your other devices listen to it and to send its audio.
  4. Closing the window keeps AudioNet running in the system tray (press Windows+B, then the arrow keys, to reach its icon). Press Enter on the icon to open the window, or the Applications key for a menu with Exit. You can turn this off, or start AudioNet minimized to the tray, in Settings (the Settings button in the window, or Settings in the tray menu).

AudioNet keeps itself up to date: new versions download, install and restart by themselves when no one is listening through the computer. You only download it by hand once.

The app is built for screen readers with standard Windows controls and spoken status announcements. It has passed automated UI Automation checks; testing with NVDA, JAWS and Narrator is still to be done, and reports are welcome. The zip also contains audionet.exe, command-line tools for direct network streaming and diagnostics.

Mac (macOS 14 Sonoma or later, Apple silicon and Intel)

AudioNet 0.8.1 for Mac, zip file, about 16 megabytes

SHA-256 checksum: c26aa9b23faeeab19e5c181e87150894af89d3f5a029dd3e66ee6f37cb5fee8d

Install

  1. Download the zip file. Safari unpacks it into AudioNet in your Downloads folder; otherwise open the zip file to unpack it.
  2. Move AudioNet to your Applications folder: in Finder, select AudioNet and press Command+C, press Command+Shift+A to go to Applications, then press Command+Option+V.
  3. Open AudioNet from Applications. It is signed and notarized by Apple, so it opens like any other app.
  4. Sign in with the server address, your AudioNet account name and password, and a name for this Mac. The Mac is then online in your account; turn on Share This Mac's Audio to let your other devices listen to it and to send its audio. macOS asks once for microphone access, and for permission to record system audio the first time another device listens to what this Mac plays.

While sharing, a Mac offers its microphones and the sound it plays (macOS 14.2 or later); its outputs can be sent to either way. Closing the window keeps it running in the menu bar, without a Dock icon (with VoiceOver, press VO-M twice to reach it; opening AudioNet again brings its window back). In AudioNet Settings (Command+comma, or Settings in its menu bar menu) you can have it start when you log in, and start in the menu bar. It keeps itself up to date like the Windows app.

The Mac app uses standard controls, with VoiceOver announcements for changes and a readable status log. It passes Xcode's automated accessibility audit; testing with VoiceOver by people who use it is still to be done, and reports are welcome.
